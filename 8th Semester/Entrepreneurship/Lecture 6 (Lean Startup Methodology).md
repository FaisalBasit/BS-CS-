Lean Startup Methodology
The Lean Startup methodology, developed by Eric Ries, is a
framework for building and managing startups based on principles
of continuous innovation, validated learning, and rapid iteration.
Key Principles of the Lean Startup
Key principles of the Lean Startup approach along with examples:
Build-Measure-Learn: Instead of spending significant time and
resources developing a product without feedback, the Lean
Startup advocates for quickly building a minimum viable product
(MVP), measuring its performance, and learning from user
feedback to make informed decisions.
For example, Dropbox initially created a simple MVP—a video
demonstrating how the product would work—before fully
developing the file-sharing platform. They measured user interest
through sign-ups and feedback and learned that there was
significant demand for their product.
Key Principles of the Lean Startup
Validated Learning: The Lean Startup emphasizes the importance
of validating assumptions and hypotheses through experiments
and data rather than relying on guesswork.
For Example, before launching Airbnb, the founders tested their
assumption that people would be willing to rent out their homes
to strangers by creating a simple website offering air mattresses
for rent during a conference. The positive response validated their
idea and encouraged them to continue developing the platform.
Key Principles of the Lean Startup
Minimum Viable Product (MVP): An MVP is the simplest version
of a product that allows a team to collect the maximum amount
of validated learning about customers with the least effort.
For example, before creating its electric cars, Tesla Motors built
the Tesla Roadster as its MVP—a high-performance electric sports
car based on a pre-existing vehicle platform. The Roadster's
success provided the foundation for further development and
expansion into the electric vehicle market.
Key Principles of the Lean Startup
Continuous Deployment: Lean Startup encourages frequent and
incremental product releases to gather feedback and iterate
quickly.
For example, Facebook regularly updates its platform with new
features and improvements based on user feedback and data
analysis. This continuous deployment allows them to respond to
user needs and market changes rapidly.
Key Principles of the Lean Startup
Pivot or Persevere: A pivot involves changing a fundamental
aspect of the business strategy based on feedback and learning.
Lean Startup advocates for being open to pivoting if the initial
approach isn't working.
For example, Instagram started as a location-based social network
called Burbn but pivoted to focus exclusively on photo sharing
after noticing that users were more interested in that feature.
Key Principles of the Lean Startup
Fail Fast, Fail Cheap: Instead of viewing failure as a negative
outcome, Lean Startup encourages embracing failure as a learning
opportunity and minimizing the cost of failure by testing ideas
quickly and inexpensively.
For example, Google's "20% time" policy allows employees to
spend a portion of their workweek on side projects, encouraging
innovation and experimentation without the fear of failure.
