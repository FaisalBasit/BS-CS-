What is Feasibility Analysis?
Feasibility analysis is the process of determining if a business
idea is viable.
It is the preliminary evaluation of a business idea, conducted for
the purpose of determining whether the idea is worth pursuing.
Feasibility analysis takes the guesswork (to a certain degree) out
of a business launch, and provides an entrepreneur with a more
secure notion that a business idea is feasible or viable.
Timing of feasibility analysis: It follows the opportunity
recognition stage and comes before the development of a
business plan.
Four Forms of Feasibility Analysis
1. Product/Service Feasibility
2. Industry/Target Market Feasibility
3. Organizational Feasibility Analysis
4. Financial Feasibility Analysis
Product/Service Feasibility
Product/Service Feasibility involves assessing the viability and potential success
of introducing a new product or service into the market.
It examines various factors such as market demand, competition, regulatory
considerations, technical feasibility, and financial feasibility to determine whether
launching the product or service is feasible and likely to be profitable.
Here's an example to illustrate product/service feasibility analysis:
Example: XYZ HealthTech is a startup company specializing in healthcare
technology. The company is considering developing a new telemedicine platform
that allows patients to schedule virtual appointments with healthcare providers and
receive remote medical consultations. Before proceeding with the development of
the platform, XYZ HealthTech conducts a product/service feasibility analysis.
Product/Service Feasibility
 Market Demand: XYZ HealthTech conducts market research to assess the
demand for telemedicine services among patients and healthcare providers. The
company analyzes factors such as demographics, healthcare trends, patient
preferences, and the availability of telemedicine solutions in the market. Market
surveys and interviews with potential users help XYZ HealthTech gauge the
level of interest and willingness to adopt the new telemedicine platform.
 Competition: The company evaluates the competitive landscape to identify
existing telemedicine platforms and healthcare providers offering similar
services. XYZ HealthTech assesses the strengths and weaknesses of
competitors, their market share, pricing strategies, and the quality of their
offerings. The analysis helps XYZ HealthTech understand the level of
competition and differentiate its telemedicine platform by offering unique
features or benefits.
Product/Service Desirability
 Regulatory Considerations: XYZ HealthTech investigates regulatory
requirements and compliance standards governing telemedicine services in the
target market. The company ensures that its platform adheres to legal and
ethical guidelines related to patient privacy, data security, medical licensing, and
telehealth regulations. Compliance with regulatory requirements is essential to
mitigate legal risks and build trust with users and healthcare providers.
 Technical Feasibility: The company assesses the technical feasibility of
developing and implementing the telemedicine platform. XYZ HealthTech
evaluates factors such as software development requirements, IT infrastructure,
compatibility with existing systems, scalability, and cybersecurity measures.
The analysis helps determine whether the company has the technical
capabilities and resources to build and maintain the platform effectively.
Industry/Target Market Feasibility
Industry/target market feasibility is an assessment of the overall appeal of the
industry and the target market of the product or service being proposed.
There is a distinct difference between a firm’s industry and its target market; having
a clear understanding of this difference is important
 An industry is a group of firms producing a similar product or service, such as
computers, children’s toys, airplanes, or social networks.
 Afirm’s target market is the portion of the industry that it goes after or to which
it wants to appeal. Most firms, and certainly entrepreneurial start-ups, typically
do not try to service an entire industry
Instead, they select or carve out a specific target market and try to service that group
of customers particularly well.
For example, is not trying to target the entire children’s toy industry. Its target
market is the set of parents who are willing to pay a premium for educational toys
that teach kids how to code.
Industry/Target Market Feasibility
There are two components to industry/target market feasibility analysis:
1. Industry attractiveness
2. Target market attractiveness.
Industry attractiveness refers to the overall appeal and potential profitability of
the industry in which a company operates or intends to enter. It involves assessing
factors such as market size, growth rate, competition, barriers to entry, supplier
power, buyer power, and technological advancements.
Example: The smartphone industry has been highly attractive in recent years due
to its rapid growth, expanding market size, and technological advancements.
Companies like Apple, Samsung, and Huawei have thrived in this industry due to
its high profitability and continuous innovation.
Industry/Target Market Feasibility
Target market attractiveness focuses on evaluating the specific segments or
groups of customers within an industry that a company intends to serve. It involves
analyzing factors such as segment size, growth potential, purchasing power,
accessibility, and alignment with the company's capabilities and objectives.
Example: Within the smartphone industry, a company might identify the youth
segment as particularly attractive due to its large size, increasing purchasing
power, and high adoption rate of new technologies. Companies like OnePlus and
Xiaomi have successfully targeted this segment by offering high-performance
smartphones at competitive prices.
While industry attractiveness sets the broader context for strategic decision
making, target market attractiveness guides companies in identifying the most
promising opportunities within that industry and tailoring their strategies to
effectively serve those segments. Both are essential considerations for companies
aiming to achieve sustainable growth and competitive advantage.
Industry/Target Market Feasibility
Let's break down each component and provide an example:
Industry feasibility analysis involves evaluating the overall attractiveness of the
industry in which a company intends to operate. It examines factors such as market
size, growth potential, competition, regulatory environment, and technological
trends.
Example: Suppose a company is considering entering the electric vehicle (EV)
industry. It conducts an industry feasibility analysis to assess the market
dynamics, including the increasing demand for EVs due to environmental
concerns and government initiatives promoting sustainable transportation. The
analysis also considers the growing investment in EV infrastructure,
advancements in battery technology, and the emergence of new players in the
industry. Additionally, the company evaluates potential barriers to entry, such as
high capital requirements, regulatory hurdles, and competition from established
automakers.
Industry/Target Market Feasibility
Target market feasibility analysis involves evaluating the specific segments or
groups of customers within the industry that the company intends to target. It
assesses factors such as segment size, growth potential, purchasing behavior,
needs, preferences, and competition.
Example: Continuing with the EV industry example, the company conducts a
target market feasibility analysis to identify the most promising customer
segments for its electric vehicles. It segments the market based on demographic
factors (e.g., age, income, geographic location), psychographic factors (e.g.,
environmental values, lifestyle), and behavioral factors (e.g., adoption of EV
technology, willingness to pay for premium features). The analysis reveals that
there is a growing segment of environmentally-conscious consumers who
prioritize sustainability and are willing to pay a premium for electric vehicles
with long battery range and fast-charging capabilities. The company decides to
target this segment with its product offerings and tailor its marketing strategies
accordingly.
Organizational Feasibility Analysis
Organizational feasibility analysis is conducted to determine whether a
proposed business has sufficient management expertise, organizational
competence, and resources to successfully launch.
Here's an example to illustrate organizational feasibility analysis:
Example: XYZ Corporation is a medium-sized manufacturing company that
specializes in producing automotive parts. The company is considering expanding
its product line to include electric vehicle components, which represent a growing
segment in the automotive industry. Before making a final decision, XYZ
conducts an organizational feasibility analysis to determine if it has the capability
to successfully enter and compete in this new market.
Organizational Feasibility Analysis
 Management Expertise: XYZ evaluates whether its current management
team possesses the necessary expertise and experience in electric vehicle
technology, market dynamics, and strategic decision-making to lead the
expansion effort effectively.
 Organizational Structure: The Company assesses its existing organizational
structure to determine if it can accommodate the new product line. It considers
factors such as departmental responsibilities, reporting relationships, and
decision-making processes.
 HumanResources: XYZ evaluates its workforce to identify any skill gaps or
shortages that may hinder the successful implementation of the expansion
plan. It considers whether additional training, hiring, or restructuring is needed
to ensure adequate human resources for the new venture.
Financial Feasibility Analysis
Financial feasibility analysis is a process used by businesses to evaluate the
economic viability of a proposed business venture or project. It involves assessing
whether the expected financial returns from the venture are likely to justify the
investment of resources. This analysis typically considers factors such as revenue
projections, cost estimates, funding requirements, cash flow projections, and
return on investment (ROI).
Here's an example to illustrate financial feasibility analysis:
Example: ABC Tech, a software development company, is considering
developing a new mobile application designed to help users track their fitness
activities and provide personalized workout plans. Before proceeding with the
development, ABC conducts a financial feasibility analysis to determine if the
project is economically viable.
Financial Feasibility Analysis
 Revenue Projections: ABC estimates the potential revenue that the mobile
application could generate based on factors such as market size, pricing strategy,
and projected user adoption rates. Market research indicates that there is a
growing demand for fitness apps, and ABC anticipates that its app could attract a
significant number of users.
 Cost Estimates: The company identifies the costs associated with developing,
launching, and maintaining the mobile application. This includes expenses such
as software development, design, marketing, server hosting, and ongoing
maintenance. ABC also considers any potential additional costs or contingencies
that may arise during the project.
 Funding Requirements: Based on the revenue projections and cost estimates,
ABC calculates the amount of funding needed to finance the development and
launch of the mobile application. This includes both initial investment costs and
working capital requirements to sustain operations until the app starts generating
revenue.
Financial Feasibility Analysis
 Cash Flow Projections: ABC prepares cash flow projections to assess the
timing and magnitude of cash inflows and outflows associated with the project.
This helps the company evaluate its ability to meet financial obligations, such as
paying suppliers, employees, and creditors, as well as servicing any debt or
financing arrangements.
 Return on Investment (ROI): The company calculates the expected return on
investment for the project by comparing the anticipated financial gains with the
initial investment and ongoing expenses. ABC considers factors such as payback
period, net present value (NPV), and internal rate of return (IRR) to assess the
attractiveness of the investment opportunity.
