What is Competitive Analysis?
 A competitor analysis, also referred to as a competitive
analysis, is the process of identifying competitors in your
industry and researching their different marketing strategies.
 Competitors has two categories:
 Direct Competitor
 Indirect Competitor
How to Do a Competitive Analysis
 Determine who your competitors are.
 Determine what products your competitors offer.
 Research your competitors' sales tactics and results.
 Take a look at your competitors' pricing, as well as any perks(مراعات) they offer.
 Ensure you're meeting competitive shipping costs.
 Analyze how your competitors market their products.
 Take note of your competition's content strategy.
 Learn what technology stack your competitors use.
 Analyze the level of engagement on your competitors' content.
 Observe how they promote marketing content.
 Look at their social media presence, strategies, and go-to platforms.
 Perform a SWOT Analysis to learn their strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and 
threats.
Difference  Between Direct and Indirect Competitors with Examples
Direct competitors are businesses that offer similar products or services to the
same target market. They often have similar pricing, features, and target the
same customer needs. Direct competitors are typically easy to identify because
they operate in the same industry and cater to the same customer base. For
example:
 Coca-Cola and PepsiCo: Both companies produce soft drinks and compete
directly for market share in the beverage industry.
 McDonald's and Burger King: These fast-food chains offer similar types of
food and vie for customers in the same market segment.
 Nike and Adidas: Both are sportswear companies producing similar
products, such as athletic shoes and apparel, and compete for customers in
the sports industry.
Difference  Between Direct and Indirect Competitors with Examples
Indirect competitors, on the other hand, offer different products or services but still
compete for the same consumer dollars. While they may not offer identical solutions,
they fulfill a similar need or serve the same purpose. Indirect competitors can be more
challenging to identify because they may operate in different industries. Examples
include:
 Uber and public transportation: While Uber provides ridesharing services, public
transportation offers a different mode of transportation, but both compete for
commuters' transportation needs.
 Netflix and movie theaters: Netflix offers streaming services for movies and TV
shows, while movie theaters provide a physical location for watching films. Both
compete for consumers' entertainment budgets.
 Airbnb and hotels: Airbnb offers accommodations through private rentals, while
hotels offer traditional lodging options. However, both cater to travelers'
accommodation needs.

Let’s take a look at popular gaming system companies Sony and Nintendo.
1. Determine who your competitors are.
 In our example, it’s Sony vs Nintendo, but it’s also worth considering
Microsoft’s Xbox, which occupies the same general market vertical.
 This is critical for effective analysis; even if you’re focused on specific
competitors and how they compare, it’s worth considering other similar
market offerings.
2. Determine what products your competitors offer.
 PlayStation offers two PS5 versions, digital and standard, at different price
points, while Nintendo offers three versions of its console.
 Bothcompanies also sell peripherals — for example, Sony sells virtual reality
(VR) add-ons, while Nintendo sells gaming peripherals such as steering
wheels, tennis rackets, and differing controller configurations.
Competitive Analysis Example
3. Research your competitors' sales tactics and results.
 When it comes to sales tactics and marketing, Sony and Nintendo have
very different approaches.
 In part thanks to the recent semiconductor shortage, Sony has driven up
demand via scarcity — very low volumes of PS5 consoles remain available.
Nintendo, meanwhile, has adopted a broader approach by targeting
families as its primary customer base.
 This effort is bolstered by the Switch Lite product line, which is smaller
and less expensive, making it a popular choice for children.
 The numbers tell the tale: Through September 2021, Nintendo sold 14.3
million consoles, while Sony sold 7.8 million.

What are the 3 C's in a competitive analysis?
The 3 C's refer to a strategic model that considers three main factors when
understanding the broader business environment:
 Company: Understanding your own strengths, weaknesses, opportunities,
and threats.
 Customers: Knowing who your target audience is, what they value, and
howthey behave.
 Competitors: Analyzing direct and indirect competitors to determine
market dynamics and potential threats.
Porter's Five Forces Strategic Model
 TheFive Forces Model was created by Harvard Business School professor,
Michael Porter and was first published in 1979.
 Porter's Five Forces model, developed by Michael Porter, is a framework
used to analyze the competitive forces that shape an industry, helping
businesses understand their competitive environment and develop
strategies to thrive within it.
 According to Porter, there are five forces that represent the key sources
of competitive pressure within an industry They are:
1. Threat of NewEntry
2. Bargaining Power of Supplier
3. Bargaining Power of Buyer
4. Threat of Substitute Products or Services:
5. Intensity of Competitive Rivalry
Porter's Five Forces Strategic Model
Threat of New Entrants: This force examines the ease with which new competitors
can enter an industry. Factors such as barriers to entry, economies of scale, brand
loyalty, and government regulations can affect the threat of new entrants.
For example:
The airline industry has high barriers to entry due to factors like high capital
requirements, regulatory approvals, and the need for extensive infrastructure. This
makes it difficult for new airlines to enter the market and compete with
established players like Delta Air Lines or American Airlines.
Bargaining Power of Suppliers: This force assesses the influence that suppliers
have over the industry in terms of pricing, quality, and availability of inputs.
Industries where suppliers have significant power can exert pressure on
profitability.
For example:
In the smartphone industry, companies like Apple and Samsung have significant
bargaining power over their suppliers of components like processors and displays.
This allows them to negotiate favorable terms and maintain control over product
quality and pricing.
Porter's Five Forces Strategic Model
Bargaining Power of Buyers: This force examines the influence that customers
have over the industry, particularly in terms of negotiating prices, demanding
higher quality, or switching to alternative products. Industries with powerful
buyers may face pressure to lower prices or improve offerings.
For example:
The retail industry experiences strong buyer power, as customers have many
options and can easily compare prices and features online. Retailers like Walmart
and Amazon must continuously innovate and offer competitive prices to attract
and retain customers.
Threat of Substitute Products or Services: This force considers the availability of
alternative products or services that could fulfill the same need as those offered
by the industry. Industries with readily available substitutes may face challenges in
retaining customers or maintaining pricing power.
For example:
The music industry has faced the threat of substitutes with the rise of digital
streaming services like Spotify and Apple Music. These services offer convenient
access to a vast library of music at affordable prices, posing a threat to traditional
sales of physical CDs or downloads.
Porter's Five Forces Strategic Model
Intensity of Competitive Rivalry: This force assesses the level of competition
among existing players in the industry. Factors such as the number of
competitors, industry growth rate, differentiation of products, and exit
barriers contribute to the intensity of rivalry.
For example:
The soft drink industry experiences intense competitive rivalry, with major
players like Coca-Cola and PepsiCo constantly battling for market share
through advertising, product innovation, and pricing strategies. This
competition often leads to aggressive marketing campaigns and frequent
product launches.
SWOT Analysis
 SWOT analysis is a strategic planning tool used to identify and evaluate
the Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats facing a business
or project. It helps organizations understand their internal capabilities and
external environment, enabling them to make informed decisions and
develop effective strategies.
 By conducting a SWOT analysis, organizations can identify their key
strengths and weaknesses and assess the external opportunities and
threats they face. This analysis serves as a foundation for strategic
planning, helping businesses capitalize on their strengths, address
weaknesses, exploit opportunities, and mitigate threats to achieve their
objectives and maintain a competitive advantage.
Component of SWOT Analysis
Strengths: Strengths are internal attributes and resources that give an
organization a competitive advantage and contribute to its success. These can
include aspects such as strong brand reputation, skilled workforce, unique
technology, or efficient processes.
Examples of strengths:
 Apple's strong brand image and loyal customer base give it a competitive edge
in the technology industry.
 Toyota's renowned reputation for quality and reliability enhances its position
in the automotive market.
 Google's vast resources and innovative culture enable it to develop cutting
edge products and services ahead of competitors.
Component of SWOT Analysis
Weaknesses: Weaknesses are internal factors that hinder an organization's
performance or put it at a disadvantage compared to competitors. These can
include areas such as limited financial resources, outdated technology, or
inexperienced management.
Examples of weaknesses:
 Asmall startup may lack the financial resources to invest in marketing and
expansion efforts, limiting its growth potential.
 A company with inefficient processes and outdated technology may
struggle to compete with more agile competitors.
 A restaurant with inconsistent service and quality issues may struggle to
retain customers and maintain profitability.
.
Component of SWOT Analysis
Opportunities: Opportunities are external factors or trends that could positively
impact the organization if leveraged effectively. These can include market trends,
technological advancements, or changes in regulations.
Examples of opportunities:
 The growing demand for renewable energy presents opportunities for
companies in the solar power industry to expand their market presence.
 The rise of e-commerce creates opportunities for traditional retailers to
develop online platforms and reach a broader customer base.
 Changes in consumer preferences towards healthier food options create
opportunities for food companies to introduce new product lines
Component of SWOT Analysis
Threats: Threats are external factors or challenges that could potentially harm
the organization's performance or even lead to its failure. These can include
competitive pressures, economic downturns, or shifts in consumer behavior.
Examples of threats:
 Intense competition in the smartphone market poses a threat to smaller
players who struggle to differentiate their products and compete on price.
 Economic recessions can lead to decreased consumer spending, affecting
businesses across various industries, such as retail and hospitality.
 Regulatory changes and government policies can pose threats to industries
such as healthcare or finance, requiring organizations to adapt to new
compliance requirements.
