## 1. VALUE ANALYSIS

### What is it?
- One of the **major techniques of cost reduction**
- A disciplined approach that ensures **necessary functions at minimum cost** without compromising on **quality, reliability, performance, and appearance**
- Also known as: **Value Engineering**, **Value Assurance**, **Value Management**

### Core Idea (Flowchart)
```
Value Analysis → Function of Product/Services → Required Function  → VALUE
                                               → Lowest Overall Cost → VALUE
```

### What is "Value"?
> Remember: **P-R-A-S-S-R**

- **P**erformance
- **R**eliability
- **A**ppearance
- **S**ervice Life
- **S**afety
- **R**ange of Operation

### Aims of Value Analysis (5 points)
1. **Simplify** the product
2. Use **cheaper and better** materials
3. **Improve** product design
4. **Reduce** the product cost
5. **Increase** the profits

### When to Apply Value Analysis? (5 triggers)
1. Company products are **losing market** / decline in sales
2. Products are **priced higher** than competitors
3. **Manufacturing cost is high**
4. **Decreasing profitability**
5. Company is **unable to meet delivery schedules**

### Uses of Value Analysis (5 benefits)
1. **Reduces** cost of product
2. **Balances** cost and performance
3. **Motivates employees** to come out with creative ideas
4. **Helps satisfy customers** with company's product
5. **Prevents over-design** of components

## 2. BUSINESS ORGANIZATIONS

### Definition
- A setup to perform specific functions to **generate profit** and provide **service**
- Functions under **relevant laws** (Companies Act 2017)
- Must register with statutory body (e.g., PEC for civil engineering)

### Entrepreneurship
- **Entrepreneur** = person who takes **risk** to start business for **profit + service**
- **Entrepreneurship** = process to **plan, design, launch, manage** a business

### 4 Types of Entrepreneurships

| Type | Key Feature |
|------|------------|
| **Small Business** | Privately owned, fewer employees, less income |
| **Scalable Medium** | Can grow without being hurt by increased demand |
| **Large Business** | 500+ employees, manufacturing/industries |
| **Social Entrepreneurs** | Community/social service (e.g., Grameen Bank, SSUET, SKMH) |

---

### 4 Types of Business Organizations

#### A) Sole Proprietorship (1 owner)
> Simplest form — ONE person does everything

**Merits:**
1. Easy to start & close
2. Quick decisions
3. Small capital needed
4. Close customer contact
5. Business secrets stay safe
6. No labor disputes

**Demerits:**
1. No division of labor
2. No large-scale advantages
3. High production cost
4. Limited machine use
5. Less credit worthiness
6. Uncertain continuity

---

#### B) Partnership (2+ persons)
> Share profits AND losses in agreed proportion

**Merits:**
1. Easy to set up
2. More capital available
3. Large production possible
4. Use of diverse talents
5. Promptness + checks on hasty decisions
6. Personal interest and initiative

**Demerits:**
1. Lack of permanency
2. Lack of mutual confidence
3. Unlimited liability
4. Delay in decisions
5. Difficult to close down

---

#### C) Private Limited Company (2–50 shareholders)
- Liability **limited** to extent of shares
- **Cannot** raise funds from stock exchange
- Privately held small business entity

---

#### D) Public Limited Company / Joint Stock Company (unlimited shareholders)
- Shares offered to **general public** via stock market
- Listed on stock exchange (e.g., KESC, PTCL)
- **Can** raise funds through shares and bonds
- Needs minimum **7 promoters**
- Must submit **annual report** to Registrar of Companies
- Also called **corporate form** of business

**Merits:**
1. Can mobilize large resources
2. More permanency
3. Specialized management
4. Easy to collect capital
5. Transferable shares
6. Suitable for large enterprise

**Demerits:**
1. Ownership vs management gap
2. Shareholders lack of interest
3. Exploitation of majority shareholders
4. Heavy taxes
5. Encourages speculation
6. Corruption & labor disputes possible

## 3. THEORY OF DEPRECIATION & DEPLETION

### Key Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Asset** | Anything owned from business point of view |
| **Tangible Asset** | Physical existence + value (machinery, building, pipes) |
| **Intangible Asset** | No physical form but has value (trademark, goodwill, skill, knowledge) |
| **Natural Asset** | Land, water, air, minerals, mines |

### Depreciation vs Depletion

|  | Depreciation | Depletion |
|--|-------------|-----------|
| **What** | Decrease in worth of **property/asset** | Decrease in worth of **natural asset** |
| **Cause** | Wear & tear, deterioration, obsolescence | Extraction/use of natural resources |
| **Example** | Machine losing value over years | Water reducing in dam, mine running out |

### Purpose of Depreciation/Depletion
1. To **recover capital** that was invested
2. To work out the **real cost** of assets

### Two Types of Depreciation

| Type | Meaning | Cause |
|------|---------|-------|
| **Physical** | Loss of **physical ability** to perform | **Wear & tear** — related to time & maintenance |
| **Functional** | Loss of **demand** for the asset | **Technology advancement** (e.g., Pentium 2 → Pentium 5) |

### Important Values — BV, MV, SV

| Value | What it means | Key point |
|-------|--------------|-----------|
| **Book Value (BV)** | Original cost − total depreciation charged | Current worth **on company records**; decreases yearly |
| **Market Value (MV)** | Price if sold on **open market** | Can differ from BV; land **appreciates** while BV depreciates |
| **Salvage Value (SV)** | Net residual value after asset is **fully used** | Sale price at end of useful life |

### 3 Methods to Calculate Depreciation

| Method | Key Feature |
|--------|------------|
| **Straight Line (SLD)** | Constant amount each year; BV reduces **linearly** |
| **Declining Balance (DB)** | Higher depreciation in **early years** |
| **Sum of Year Digits (SYD)** | Rapid write-off; most value gone in **first 1/3 of life** |

### SLD Key Idea
- Same depreciation **every year**
- D = (Initial Cost − Salvage Value) / Life of Asset
- Book Value after m years = Initial Cost − (m × D)

### SYD Key Idea
- **Larger** depreciation in early years, **smaller** in later years
- Most of the asset value written off in the **first 1/3rd** of its life
- Uses sum of digits (1+2+3+...+n) as denominator

## 4. CAPITAL FINANCING & ALLOCATION

### Definition
- Process of **allocation and distribution of financial resources** for **long-term financial stability**
- Purpose: **value addition** + **fair returns** to capital providers while covering risks

### Key Points
- Capital decisions → **higher management level** and **country policy level**
- Investment decisions are **strategic/policy decisions**
- Invest where yield is **positive** or better than **MARR** (Minimum Attractive Rate of Return)
- Always **anticipate risks** before investing — what can affect **cash inflow/outflow**?

### 3 Modes of Financing

| Mode | How it works |
|------|-------------|
| **Self-investment** | Investor uses own money |
| **Shareholders** | Equity from multiple investors |
| **Borrowing** | Bank/financial institution — lease/credit with compound interest |

### Budget/Financial Control
- Ensures **financial discipline** for reliable capital financing

### Benefit Cost Ratio (B/C or BCR)
- Used to **evaluate projects** — are benefits greater than costs?
- **B/C > 1** → Project is **DESIRABLE** ✓
- **B/C < 1** → Project is **NOT desirable** ✗

| Method | Formula | Note |
|--------|---------|------|
| **Conventional** | B/C = B ÷ (CR + O&M) | Older method; cannot calculate dis-benefits |
| **Modified** | B/C = (B − DB − O&M) ÷ CR | Newer; accounts for **dis-benefits (DB)** |

> **CR** = Capital Recovery Cost (initial investment converted to annual cost)
> **O&M** = Operation & Maintenance expenses

## 5. GOODS MARKET & MONEY MARKET

### Goods Market
- All **buying and selling** of goods & services
- Households = **buyers**, Companies = **sellers**
- Determines **equilibrium level of output**

### Money Market
- Interaction between **demand for money** and **supply of money**
- Supply controlled by **State Bank** through banking system
- Determines **equilibrium interest rate**
- Provides **short-term liquidity** to governments, banks, organizations
- Securities trade in **very high denominations**
- It's a **dealer/trader market** — firms trade at their own risk

### Key Difference
| | Goods Market | Money Market |
|-|-------------|-------------|
| **Determines** | Equilibrium **output** | Equilibrium **interest rate** |
| **Players** | Households & companies | Banks, government, large organizations |
| **What's traded** | Goods & services | Money/securities |

## 6. MARKET STRUCTURE (4 Types)

> Memory trick: **P-M-O-M** (Perfect, Monopoly, Oligopoly, Monopolistic)

| Structure | # of Firms | Key Features |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Perfect Competition** | 100s of firms | Free entry/exit, homogeneous goods, perfect information, **normal profit** |
| **Monopoly** | 1 firm (≥25% market share) | Barriers to entry, higher prices, **economies of scale** |
| **Oligopoly** | Few large firms (5-firm ratio >50%) | Firms are **interdependent**, some barriers to entry |
| **Monopolistic Competition** | Several firms with brand loyalty | Low barriers, **differentiated products**, less profit than monopoly |
