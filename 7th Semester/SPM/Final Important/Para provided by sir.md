# MY PROJECT MANAGEMENT STRATEGY

Building a XXXXX project is a software project that naturally involves uncertainty, evolving requirements, and the need for strong communication and coordination. As the project manager, my role would revolve around applying the core management responsibilities to ensure the project stays on track even as customer feedback changes the requirements.

To begin with, effective stakeholder expectation management is essential. Management includes representing and liaising with users., which means I must communicate clearly with stakeholders about what the team can realistically deliver within the tight deadlines. Since software projects often face uncertain and evolving requirements, I would set expectations early by explaining that the software may need to be refined iteratively as real users interact with it. Regular updates, short demos, and feedback sessions would help keep stakeholders aligned while preventing unrealistic demands from building up.

Next, to coordinate the development team, I would rely on the core management activities listed in the chapter—planning, organizing, directing, monitoring, and controlling. Because the team is small, communication must be simple and clear. I would break work into manageable tasks, clarify responsibilities, and hold short daily check‑ins to keep everyone synchronized.software development is a cognitive and document‑heavy process that requires strong communication and coordination), so the PM must remove blockers quickly and maintain a supportive environment that enables the team to focus.

Managing changing requirements is also a key responsibility. Planning is an iterative process and  monitoring and controlling help the PM respond to change and correct deviations when needed. To handle evolving software features, I would maintain a prioritized list of requirements, evaluate the impact of each new suggestion on time and cost, and update the plan accordingly. No change would be accepted without checking whether the team can realistically complete it without jeopardizing deadlines. This lightweight yet controlled approach ensures that flexibility does not turn into chaos.

Finally, because communication is one of the biggest challenges in software projects—especially due to lack of clarity and high uncertainty—I would make sure all project information (requirements, decisions, and progress) is documented and shared transparently. Establishing a single source of truth helps avoid misunderstandings and keeps the team and stakeholders aligned.

In summary, by applying the foundational responsibilities of a project manager -communicating clearly, planning iteratively, coordinating the team, managing change, and continually monitoring progress—I can guide the software project through shifting requirements while keeping both the team and stakeholders confident and aligned.
