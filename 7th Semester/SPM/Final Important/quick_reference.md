## Procurement Contracts

### What to Do:
1. **Memorize 3 contract types**:
   - Fixed Price: Clear scope, risk on contractor
   - Reimbursable (Cost Plus): Unclear scope, risk on client
   - Time & Material: Undefined scope, shared risk
2. **Know subdivisions** (FFP, FPIF, CPFF, CPIF, T&M variations)
3. **Practice scenarios**: Development vs Support contracts
4. **Understand when multiple contracts** might be needed

### Key Focus:
- Scenario analysis (which contract to use?)
- Provide 1-2 examples per type
- Justify contract selection based on scope, risk, budget
- Complex scenarios may need TWO contracts

## Earned Value Management

### What to Do:
1. **Master ALL formulas**:
   - PV, EV, AC
   - SPI = EV/PV, CPI = EV/AC
   - SV = EV - PV, CV = EV - AC
2. **Focus on COMPLEX scenarios** (not simple ones)
3. **Practice interpretation**: SPI/CPI > 1 = good, < 1 = bad
4. **Understand professor's hint**: Study the "complex end" - which side to read from?
5. **Practice multi-step problems** with multiple phases

### Key Focus:
- Complex calculations weighted MORE heavily
- Interpretation of performance indices
- Forecasting and estimation
- VERY IMPORTANT topic!

## Maslow & Herzberg

### What to Do:
1. **Study Lecture 14** - "Managing People in Software Environment" (end section)
2. **Memorize Maslow's 5 levels**: Physiological → Safety → Social → Esteem → Self-Actualization
3. **Understand Herzberg's 2 factors**:
   - Hygiene (salary, policies, conditions) = prevent dissatisfaction
   - Motivation (achievement, recognition, growth) = true motivation
4. **Practice scenario questions**:
   - Person has everything but unhappy → which need unmet?
   - How to motivate someone with all material comforts?

### Key Focus:
- Practical application to PM scenarios
- Why people are unmotivated despite good salary/benefits
- Not overly complex questions
- Professor's favorite theories

## Project Cost Management

### What to Do:
1. **Understand the baseline formula**:
   - `Baseline = Direct Costs + Risk Reserves`
2. **Know cost components**:
   - Direct: HR, Machines, Services, Materials
   - Reserves: Contingency + Management reserves
3. **Practice extracting values** from tables
4. **Calculate risk reserves** using Risk Management formulas
5. **Remember**: SPI/CPI are NOT in Cost Management (they're in EVM)

### Key Focus:
- Numerical calculations
- Tabular data extraction
- Simple addition and multiplication
- Show breakdown of cost components

## Stakeholder Register

### What to Do:
1. **Review Chapter 3/4** on Power-Interest Grid
2. **Know table columns**:
   - Name/Role, Power Level, Interest Level, Focus Area, Update Requirements
3. **Memorize 4 quadrants**:
   - High Power/High Interest → Manage Closely
   - High Power/Low Interest → Keep Satisfied
   - Low Power/High Interest → Keep Informed
   - Low Power/Low Interest → Monitor
4. **Practice creating tables** from scenarios

### Key Focus:
- Table creation based on scenario
- Identify stakeholder power and interest levels
- Determine focus areas and update requirements
- Find example in lecture slides

## Organizational Structure

### What to Do:
1. **Memorize 3 main structures**: Functional, Projectized, Matrix
2. **Focus on Matrix types**:
   - Weak (FM: 70-80%, PM: 20-30%)
   - Balanced (FM: 50%, PM: 50%) → HIGHEST conflict
   - Strong (FM: 20-30%, PM: 70-80%)
3. **Understand conflict reasons**: Dual reporting, competing priorities
4. **Know authority levels** for PM vs Functional Manager in each type
5. **Be ready to explain** responsibility division

### Key Focus:
- Structure identification from scenarios
- Matrix structure variations (most important)
- Why Balanced Matrix has highest conflicts
- Authority percentages
- Very straightforward topic
