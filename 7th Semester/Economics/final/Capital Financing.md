Capital Financing and Allocation  
  
Capital financing and allocation is the process of allocation and distribution of financial 
resources with a purpose of enhancing long-term financial stability  
   
Another purpose of Capital financing and allocation is value addition from allocation of 
finances to provide fair returns to providers of capital covering risks  
  
Capital allocation decisions are made at higher level by the management and at country 
level by the policy makers  
  
Decision of financial investment are considered a strategic or policy decision  
  
Decision to invest must be arrived after considering all relevant aspects like where investment 
to produce positive yield or better MARR  
Capital financing is the process through which a investor uses judgment to determine 
whether to invest or decline from investment based on the merits and de-merits  
  
What could be the possible risks associated with investment that can be expected at the time 
of investment. It is necessary that risks may be anticipated and efforts made to address risks 
prior to investment together with what obstacles can affect cash inflow or cash out flow?  
  
Mode of financing could be  investment by investor or with  shareholder  or borrowing from 
Bank or any financial institution like lease or credit to pay back with compound interest.  
  
Budget or financial control is also included in Capital Financing and Allocation to ensure 
financial discipline for a reliable Capital Financing and Allocation.

Goods Market and Money Market Economy  
  
Goods Market  
By Goods Market we mean all the buying and selling of goods and services.   
  
Goods markets are markets in which companies and households interact to 
buy and sell the output of goods and services.  
  
In this market households act as buyers, while companies act as sellers.   
  
This  role  in  Goods  Market  changes  in  money  market  where  production  and 
transaction takes place.  
  
The equilibrium level of output is determined in the goods market.   
  
Money Market   
By Money Market we mean the interaction between demand for money and 
the supply of money (the size of the money stock) is set by the State Bank 
working through the banking system  
  
The equilibrium interest rate is determined in the money market.   
  
The money market contributes to the economic stability and development of 
a  country  by  providing  short-term  liquidity  to  governments,  commercial 
banks, and other large organizations.   
  
Investors with excess money that they do not need can invest it in the money 
market and earn interest.  
  
One of the main differences between the money market and the stock market 
is that most money market securities trade in very high denominations.   
  
Furthermore, the money market is a trader market, which means that firms 
buy and sell securities in their own accounts, at their own risk.
